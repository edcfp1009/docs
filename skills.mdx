## The difference

**MCP tools** are the individual actions your AI client can call: tracking, quoting, booking, importing. They're documented in [Available Tools](/tools/overview). Connecting the MCP Server gives your AI client access to all of them.

**Skills** are a layer on top. Instead of the AI client figuring out on its own which tools to call and in what order for a given task, a skill gives it a pre-built playbook, the sequence FreightPOP recommends for that specific job. Tools answer "what can be called." Skills answer "in what order, and with what checks, for this task."

## Why it matters

Without a skill, asking your AI client to _"book the cheapest option to Chicago"_ still works. It calls `get_rates`, picks the lowest number, and calls `create_shipment`. But it's improvising that sequence itself, so it might skip a step you'd want it to take, like validating the destination address first.

A skill would encode FreightPOP's own recommended sequence. For example: validate the address, get rates, filter by transit time if one was mentioned, confirm with you, then book. The AI client follows that sequence instead of guessing one.

## What's planned

The tools documented in [Available Tools](/tools/overview) already cover tracking, quoting, shipping, orders, and address validation. Skills would package the recommended workflows across those tools, for example a booking skill that chains address validation, rate comparison, confirmation, and booking automatically.
